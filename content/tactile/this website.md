this is, as far as I can remember, my third iteration of a personal website. the first one was so fun (and frustrating) to build - each page built with handwritten html, learning CSS for the first time. earlier versions were professional portfolios summarizing my work and experiences. this is the first version that is purely for fun.

i decided to make a blog (ish) earlier this year, after finding and enjoying lots of other people's blogs. i didn't want to write on substack or somewhere intended for wide dissemination. for now, this is mostly for me, and the few friends i share it with. i want to write more, to keep a record of my life, the evolution of my thoughts, and to create space to more clearly articulate ideas.

### design

this iteration is much easier to maintain, written in markdown and then rendered into html, built with much help from generative AI^[largely Claude Opus 5/Cursor Composer 2.5, as stamp of the moment in time.].

i wanted a clean, easy to read foundation. the overall layout and macro/micro sections of this blog were inspired by [Tom MacWright](https://macwright.com/), whose blog Rohit introduced me to^[if you ever read this, hi Tom!]. the macro/micro distinction i found really useful to differentiate longer write ups from quick reflections.

i have always had a fondness for typography and spend way too much time in any project picking the right fonts. for this project, I loved the retro vibe of [Departure Mono](https://departuremono.com/) and the clean, readable sans serif [Standard](https://github.com/brycewilner/Standard). i couldn't decide which vibe i liked better for the site, so i added a font switcher to the bottom of the pages and built two style options (themed as **terminal** and **magazine**)

![[img/font-switcher.gif]]

on the home page, pictured above, i also really enjoyed designing the icons for macro, micro, and tactile sections (and the idea to have them in the first place). for the macro section, i always think about fractals and spirals, the way patterns repeat across scales. for micro, i pictured a close up of cells. the micro drawing also reminded me of the core of a flower, which translated perfectly for the tactile section. i've never much been one for drawing, so i was pretty psyched with how well these sketches turned out.

also fun - the drawings looked a little out of place in the **terminal** styling, so i turned them into ASCII art using an online image to ASCII converter. i simply love the vibe of the result.

for the longer essays, because i am prone to digressions, i wanted to have side notes. these take inspiration from [how Edward Tufte lays out pages](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!k55O!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4f49e634-fc0f-4eb8-9704-83b987c16fe6_2461x3018.jpeg). the side notes become hover text when the window is narrower. the longer essays also get a clickable overview on wide screens. i probably stole this from [Dario Amodei](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology), but i can't remember the direct inspiration.

![[img/macro-notes.gif]]

i also built this site to house a library of my real world projects.^[though, as you can tell from this post, it also now includes some internet stuff.] i love making things and showing them to people, and this section is probably my most referenced, in conversation when someone says "i'd love to see what you make".

i recently added support for gifs, so some of the icons move on the page, which i find incredibly fun. i'm trying to restrain my use so the page doesn't turn into an overwhelming torrent of independently moving images, but it's hard to resist the urge.
