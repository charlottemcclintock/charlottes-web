Ashish’s yoga class today was so beautiful. he took the Halloween theme and brought it a contemplation on meeting death and fear with levity, humor, and joy. how our fear of death is rooted in an aversion to being complete. how yoga teaches us that we are complete, now, in this moment. death as a reminder of the joy of being alive. 

he also reflected on how our greatest fear is often not belonging, standing out from the crowd. his comments on belonging helped frame my thoughts on a year spent connecting more deeply with new people, and anticipatory nerves as I head on a work trip alone to meet strangers. belonging is so primal. entering new spaces with strangers creates this deep anxiety that we won’t be welcomed, that we won’t belong (see also: [hospitality as a lost art](/micro/hospitality-as-a-lost-art)).^[Leah at the studio is particularly good at welcoming. She greets everyone with such genuine warmth and presence, and it sets a powerful tone for the culture.] after class, Ashish and I were talking about his experience finding a sense of belonging in yoga studios. I also think finding a deep sense of belonging in one space lets you carry that feeling with you beyond.
